## 1. Executive Summary

The client expects the project to deliver more than a collection of static figures prepared for a demonstration. The expected final outcome is a reproducible and explainable analysis workflow that can process the supplied OpenET2 data, reproduce relevant methods and results from the client-provided research paper, generate structured analytical outputs, create publication-quality figures, and present the results through a clear HTML report.

The analysis must preserve the distinction between accuracy, spread or precision, and empirical 95% ellipse coverage. These measurements should not be combined into an arbitrary composite score. The workflow should also compare the primary onset-compensation candidates of 50 ms, 100 ms, and 150 ms, with the wider 0–200 ms range retained only as secondary sensitivity analysis.

The current recommendation of 50 ms must be presented as a result for the supplied dataset under the agreed selection rule. It must not be described as a universal human reaction time.

## 2. Overall Client Feedback

### 2.1 The project direction is appropriate but requires stronger productisation

The existing Demo B work demonstrates meaningful progress in data analysis, paper reproduction, visualisation, and reporting. However, the final project should be organised as a coherent and reusable workflow rather than as a set of disconnected scripts, figures, and presentation materials.

The final solution should:

- provide a clear entry point for running the analysis;
- use documented and repeatable processing steps;
- retain the parameters used for each result;
- generate both machine-readable and human-readable outputs;
- explain how conclusions were derived;
- support verification by another team member or stakeholder; and
- be capable, where practical, of processing future data with the same structure.

### 2.2 The reproduced results must be methodologically defensible

The client expects the team to reproduce the analytical work described in the supplied paper, not merely create visually similar figures.

The team should:

- identify the research questions and analytical objectives in the paper;
- map the supplied dataset to the variables used in the paper;
- reproduce the relevant preprocessing and filtering logic;
- document any assumptions required because of missing information;
- reproduce the required analytical results and figures;
- compare the reproduced results with the published results; and
- investigate and explain material differences.

### 2.3 The final presentation must be internally consistent

The PowerPoint slides, HTML report, CSV outputs, generated figures, code, and spoken conclusions must describe the same:

- data source;
- test conditions;
- delay values;
- selected delay;
- metric definitions;
- number of sessions or participants;
- analytical assumptions;
- limitations; and
- final recommendation.

Any result updated in one output must also be updated in all other relevant outputs.

## 3. Paper Reproduction Requirements

### 3.1 Required analytical alignment

The reproduction workflow should follow the client-provided paper as the primary methodological reference. The team must identify and document:

- the purpose of each analysis;
- the unit of analysis;
- the relevant participant, session, condition, target, and recording identifiers;
- the data-selection and exclusion rules;
- the preprocessing sequence;
- the coordinate transformations;
- the statistical calculations;
- the ellipse-generation method;
- the figure-generation method; and
- the interpretation of the reported results.

### 3.2 Comparison with the paper

For each reproduced result, the team should record:

- the corresponding figure, table, or method in the paper;
- the data used by the project;
- the parameters used in the reproduction;
- whether the result is directly comparable;
- the observed similarities and differences;
- any limitations affecting comparability; and
- the team's explanation for material differences.

### 3.3 Assumptions and limitations

Assumptions must be stated explicitly. These may include:

- assumed field of view;
- missing screen dimensions or viewing distance;
- inferred target ordering;
- inferred coordinate mapping;
- missing preprocessing details;
- interpretation of recording identifiers;
- treatment of invalid or missing gaze samples;
- minimum valid-window requirements; and
- statistical or visual quality-control thresholds.

Assumptions must not be presented as facts supplied by the client unless the client has explicitly confirmed them.

## 4. Delay Analysis Requirements

### 4.1 Primary candidates

The primary onset-compensation candidates are:

- `0.0500 s` or 50 ms;
- `0.1000 s` or 100 ms; and
- `0.1500 s` or 150 ms.

These three values must be compared directly and presented prominently.

### 4.2 Secondary sensitivity analysis

A wider scan from `0.0000 s` to `0.2000 s` may be retained to show sensitivity across the complete range. This scan is secondary evidence and must not replace the primary comparison of 50, 100, and 150 ms.

The primary and secondary analyses should be visually and textually distinguished.

### 4.3 Selection rule

The selected delay must be justified using separate accuracy and spread results.

The agreed selection principle is:

1. Begin with the shortest primary candidate.
2. Move to a longer candidate only when it provides a clear improvement.
3. Do not move to a longer candidate when improvement in one metric conflicts with deterioration in another.
4. When the candidates perform similarly, select the shorter candidate.

Statistical comparison, such as paired participant-level bootstrap analysis, should be used where appropriate to determine whether a longer candidate provides a clear, non-conflicting improvement.

### 4.4 Interpretation of the selected delay

The recommended delay must be described carefully.

Acceptable wording:

> For the supplied dataset and the agreed accuracy-and-spread selection rule, 50 ms was selected as the preferred onset-compensation value.

Unacceptable wording:

> Human reaction time is 50 ms.

The project result is dataset-specific and should not be generalised beyond the available evidence.

## 5. Metric Requirements

### 5.1 Accuracy

Accuracy should measure how close the gaze estimate is to the intended target.

The report should state:

- the exact accuracy formula;
- the coordinate unit;
- the aggregation level;
- the treatment of repeated markers;
- the participant-weighting method; and
- whether lower values indicate better performance.

### 5.2 Spread or precision

Spread should measure the dispersion or repeatability of gaze observations independently from target accuracy.

The report should state:

- the spread definition;
- whether it is radial, axis-specific, or ellipse-based;
- the aggregation method;
- how repeated recordings are handled; and
- whether lower values indicate better repeatability.

### 5.3 Empirical 95% ellipse coverage

Empirical 95% coverage should be reported as a separate quality-assurance result.

The report should explain:

- how the ellipse is calculated;
- what population of points it represents;
- how empirical coverage is calculated;
- why observed coverage may differ from exactly 95%; and
- why coverage is not included in an arbitrary delay-selection score.

### 5.4 Ellipse centre error

The ellipse centre should be compared with the intended target position. This metric should remain separate from ellipse spread.

The structured output should retain:

- target coordinates;
- ellipse-centre coordinates;
- centre-to-target error;
- condition;
- recording or session identifier; and
- selected delay.

### 5.5 Ellipse area

Ellipse area may be used as a supporting spread or quality metric. It must be labelled clearly and must not be confused with accuracy.

### 5.6 Prohibition on arbitrary composite scoring

Accuracy, spread, and empirical coverage must not be combined into a composite score unless the client later approves a documented and justified scoring method.

The default reporting model is:

- accuracy reported separately;
- spread reported separately;
- empirical coverage reported separately; and
- delay selection justified by explicit comparison and the shortest-equivalent rule.

## 6. Test Condition Requirements

Results must be available separately for:

1. Calibration or calibration-and-accuracy testing;
2. the 10-minute accuracy condition; and
3. the 60-minute accuracy condition.

For each condition, the project should provide:

- structured results;
- at least one readable summary figure;
- relevant target-level or recording-level evidence;
- the delay used;
- the number of valid observations;
- the accuracy result;
- the spread result; and
- relevant ellipse quality-assurance results.

An overall combined summary may be provided in addition to the separate condition results, but it must not replace them.

## 7. Visualisation Requirements

### 7.1 Marker consistency

Markers representing equivalent observations must have equal visual area.

Different recordings, dates, or categories should be distinguished using shape, fill, or another unambiguous visual encoding. Suitable examples include:

- hollow circles;
- filled circles;
- stars;
- diamonds; or
- other clearly documented shapes.

Marker size alone should not be used to distinguish recordings when the markers represent equivalent observation units.

### 7.2 Target mapping

The team must verify:

- the coordinates of all targets;
- target identifiers;
- target ordering;
- target labels;
- the mapping between data events and targets; and
- consistency between figures and structured outputs.

Any corrected target mapping should be stored in a reusable table or configuration file.

### 7.3 Standalone readability

Each important figure should be understandable when opened independently.

Each figure should identify:

- the analytical topic;
- the test condition;
- the delay value or delay range;
- the selected delay, where relevant;
- axis labels and units;
- the meaning of marker shapes and colours;
- the sample or observation count, where useful; and
- the result or interpretation supported by the figure.

### 7.4 Required figure categories

The final package should include, where relevant:

- primary 50/100/150 ms accuracy comparison;
- primary 50/100/150 ms spread comparison;
- secondary 0–200 ms sensitivity curves;
- separate Calibration, 10-minute, and 60-minute result figures;
- target-level evidence figures;
- recording-level comparison figures;
- empirical coverage quality-assurance figures;
- ellipse centre-error figures; and
- ellipse area or spread figures.
